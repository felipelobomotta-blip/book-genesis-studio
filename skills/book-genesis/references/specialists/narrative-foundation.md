# Narrative foundation specialist

Builds what Phase 1: Foundation delivers: characters with psychological depth, theme as a question, the voice specification with its Rhythm contract, and the chapter-by-chapter emotional curve.

When to load: Phase 1: Foundation (orchestrator) to write artifacts/03-characters.md, artifacts/04-theme.md, artifacts/05-voice.md and artifacts/06-emotional-curve.md; Phase 2: Architecture (orchestrator) to mark scene pressure and pacing in artifacts/07-outline.md; Phase 3: Drafting (writer) for the voice cards and the Rhythm contract; Phase 4: Adversarial Audit (auditor) and Phase 5: Revision Loop (revision editor) for the voice and rhythm tests. Never give this file or artifacts/05-voice.md to the blind reader.

The foundation sets the book's ceiling. Brilliant prose with shallow characters is forgettable; deep characters with an empty theme are disposable; emotion without theme is melodrama. Build the four artifacts together, because each informs the others. Numbers marked "house default" are Book Genesis conventions, not market findings: where a comp measurement in artifacts/01-market-map.md differs, the comp wins.

## 1. Characters (artifacts/03-characters.md)

### Seven layers

Build every main character from the inside out.

1. **Wound.** The formative event or pattern that shaped how they see the world. It need not appear on the page; the writer must know it. Ask: what happened to them that they never got over?
2. **Lie.** The false belief the wound created. They act on it until the arc forces a confrontation. Wound: abandonment. Lie: "if I am perfect, nobody leaves."
3. **Conscious desire.** What they think they want. Concrete and visible: get the job, cure the anxiety, save the brother.
4. **Unconscious need.** What they actually need; it almost always conflicts with the desire. Desire: "I want to be cured." Need: "I need to accept there is no cure, only constancy." The arc happens when the need overrides the desire, or fails to (tragedy).
5. **Contradictions.** At least two per main character. Generous with strangers, cruel at home; brave in public, cowardly in private; preaches honesty, lies to himself.
6. **Voice.** How they speak and think, specified in their voice card in artifacts/05-voice.md (section 3.4 below).
7. **Arc.** Positive: overcomes the lie and embraces the need. Negative: the lie wins. Flat: the character holds and the world around them changes (rare and hard).

### Noise profile

People are not plot vehicles. For each main character, record the human noise the prose can show without justification:

- **Irrelevant preoccupation:** something they care about that has nothing to do with the plot (how a dishwasher should be loaded, a half-finished jigsaw on the dining table).
- **Habitual thinking error:** catastrophizing, all-or-nothing thinking, mind-reading. Mundane and daily, unlike the structural lie.
- **Intrusive memory:** concrete, sensory, emotionally loaded, unconnected to the scene where it surfaces.
- **Where control fails:** a mundane trigger, not a climactic one (crying in a supermarket, laughing in a serious meeting).

House default dose: literary fiction and memoir, at least one noise moment per chapter; commercial fiction, one every two or three chapters; never more than two or three in a single chapter, counting the writer's and the revision editor's together. Prescriptive non-fiction gives the author persona no noise profile, because it undermines credibility; narrative non-fiction gives it to the case-study subjects, not to the author. The revision moves in references/specialists/prose-craft.md draw on this profile.

### Memoir and non-fiction

The author is the protagonist, and the seven layers apply to the author-narrator: the wound is the triggering event (crisis, diagnosis, loss); the lie is what they believed; the desire is what they sought; the need is what they actually needed; the contradictions are where they sabotaged themselves; the voice is how they tell it; the arc runs from where they started to where they arrived. Secondary real people (family, doctors, friends) need at least wound, voice and narrative function: what they represent in the protagonist's story.

### Secondary characters

Anyone in three or more chapters, or with a speaking role in a pivotal scene, gets a mini profile: role in the protagonist's life; their own problem, unrelated to the protagonist; one irrational behavior that is never explained; one voice marker (their mini card in artifacts/05-voice.md).

### Relationships as mirrors

Each main relationship reveals a facet the protagonist would not show alone: with the mother, vulnerability; with the therapist, resistance; with the ex, the pattern of attachment. Map every main relationship to what it shows.

```
## [Name], [role]
Wound:
Lie:
Conscious desire:
Unconscious need:
Contradictions: 1. [ ] 2. [ ]
Arc: [positive | negative | flat], from [ ] to [ ]
Noise profile: preoccupation [ ]; thinking error [ ]; intrusive memory [ ]; control fails when [ ]
Relationships: [character]: shows [facet]; pressure between them [ ]
Voice card: kept once, in artifacts/05-voice.md, card [name]; the chapter brief pulls it from there
```

For a series, align each volume's wounds, lies and arcs with the series arc in references/specialists/series.md.

## 2. Theme (artifacts/04-theme.md)

Theme is a question. The book investigates it and never answers it.

- "What is left when everything fails?" is a theme. "Resilience wins" is propaganda.
- "Why do we follow the script?" is a theme. "We must question the system" is a pamphlet.

If the reader closes the book knowing "the answer", the theme has been reduced to the moral of a fable.

### Four levels of weaving

- **Macro:** the question of the whole book. Defined once, never stated in the text, touched by every chapter.
- **Middle:** each part or act approaches the question from a different angle. For example: Part I personal (the question in the protagonist's life), Part II structural (the question in society), Part III existential (the question without an answer, and what remains).
- **Micro:** each chapter carries one aspect of the question and advances it, never repeats it. Chapter 1: "Why did I break?" Chapter 5: "Why did everyone break?" Chapter 9: "Who profits from it?"
- **Nano:** the motif system. Objects, colours and recurring actions that echo the question without being explained: forms for the impersonal system, a blinking cursor for waiting without an answer, a blank field for the emptiness nobody fills. The reader absorbs them without being told.

```
# Theme: [working title]
Question (macro):
Subthemes: [questions that branch from it]
Angles (middle): Part I [ ] / Part II [ ] / Part III [ ]
Chapter aspects (micro): one line per planned chapter
Carried by: [the choices and situations that embody the question, never statements]
Receding chapters: [which, and the motif that keeps contact]
Motifs: [2-4 motif cards]
```

Motif card:

```
Motif: [ ]
First appearance: chapter [N], [how it enters naturally]
Evolution: [how its meaning shifts]
Last appearance: chapter [N], [how it lands differently]
Link to the question: [ ]
```

Not every detail is a symbol. House default: leave 30-40% of descriptive details as plain texture. A book where every detail resonates reads as a puzzle, not a world.

### Theme anti-patterns

1. **Stated in dialogue.** "You know what I learned? That constancy is what matters." Never. Show it.
2. **Repeated without variation.** If every chapter says the same thing about the question, it is a sermon.
3. **Absent.** A chapter may recede to the nano level (a motif, no thematic pressure) so the book can breathe, and one or two chapters should. A chapter with no contact at any level does not belong in the book.
4. **Answered.** If the book ends in certainty, the theme died. The question leaves with the reader.

## 3. Voice specification (artifacts/05-voice.md)

Phase 1: Foundation owns the voice. Voice is what separates a book that reads as machine-made from one that reads as written by a person: a deep character with a generic voice is a well-drawn puppet talking like a chatbot. Build the voice by method, not by chance.

```
# Voice: [working title]
## Sources (author sample | reference authors)
## Voice definition
## Rhythm contract (baseline, envelope, pressure map, precedence, local limits, rough sentence, detection test)
## Voice cards
## Differentiation matrix
## Benchmark passages
```

### 3.1 Sources

**With an author sample** (memoir, personal non-fiction, or any author who has one):

1. Collect 3-5 pages of unguarded writing: long emails, social posts, a diary, transcribed voice notes. Writing done without trying to "write well" shows the real voice.
2. Extract: typical sentence length and structure; words they use and words they would never use; verbal tics; irony level; how they handle emotion (directly, evasively, with humor as cover, in abstractions); punctuation habits; where their comparisons come from (music, games, work, childhood).
3. Encode the patterns as rules. "Most sentences under 15 words; past 25 is deliberate." "Never writes 'profound', 'resonate', 'transcend'; writes 'brutal', 'messed up', 'heavy'." "Humor arrives at the worst moments: when things are grave he jokes, when they are light he goes serious." "References come from technology and a 1990s childhood, never from high culture."
4. Measure the sample like a comp passage (references/specialists/research.md, Step 6). It is the strongest rhythm evidence the book has. Pair it with the closest comp passage, so the auditor has a published reference point.

**Without an author sample** (fiction, ghostwriting):

1. Choose 2-3 published authors whose voice approaches the target: "Tati Bernardi's acid self-deprecation with Johann Hari's journalistic research"; "Clarice Lispector's fragmented interiority with Rubem Fonseca's dryness".
2. Decompose each: what makes the voice recognizable in one paragraph? What do we take, and what do we leave?
3. Synthesize, and add something of the book's own: a copied voice is pastiche.

Authors name the voice; the Rhythm contract pins it to titles and passages. Never infer any part of the voice from a genre or language label (references/specialists/research.md, rule 3).

### 3.2 Voice definition

```
POV and tense: [first | close third | omniscient | multiple, with rotation]; tense [ ]; rule for any shift
Vocabulary level: [1-10, where 1 = Hemingway and 10 = Nabokov]
Formality: [street | casual | conversational | measured | formal | elevated]
Humor: [none | dry | frequent | central]; when it disappears
Emotional register: [detached | restrained | open | raw]
Narrator notices first: [ ]; ignores: [ ]
Image sources: [domains the metaphors come from]; forbidden domains: [ ]
Distinctive features: [what separates this voice from generic "good writing"]
Never does: [words, constructions and moves this voice refuses]
AI-tell limits: references/patterns/ai-tells-en.md or references/patterns/ai-tells-pt-br.md as written;
  list here only book-specific exceptions, each justified by a comp passage
Rhythm: the Rhythm contract below
```

### 3.3 Rhythm contract

This is the only definition of sentence rhythm in Book Genesis. Every other file cites the Rhythm contract by name and sets no rhythm of its own. It has two layers. Copy the whole contract into artifacts/05-voice.md, including the precedence rule, the local limits, the rough sentence and the detection test: the writer's chapter brief and the auditor receive that artifact, not this file.

If research found no anchor for a layer, write `no comp evidence yet` in its Status, keep that layer neutral (plain declarative narration, no signature cadence, no invented rule), and ask the orchestrator to list the gap as an open question in PROJECT_STATE.yaml.

**Layer 1: Baseline.** How the narration sounds when no one is speaking and the outline marks the scene `rest`.

```
Baseline: [checkable description, e.g. "mean 11-15 words per sentence; simple and compound
  sentences, subordinate clauses rare; paragraphs of 3-6 sentences that open on an action
  and close on an image"]
Anchor: [comp title], [author], [edition; translator if any], [location of the passage]
Measured: [from the Sentence rhythm evidence in artifacts/01-market-map.md, same unit]
Status: [read and measured | recall, provisional | no comp evidence yet]
```

**Layer 2: Envelope.** The fastest and the slowest cadence this voice permits, each end anchored to a comp.

```
| End     | Cadence (description + measurement)                          | Anchor (comp + location) | Pressures that move toward it |
|---------|--------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------|-------------------------------|
| Fastest | [e.g. 3-8 word sentences and fragments; one-line paragraphs] | [ ]                      | [ ]                           |
| Slowest | [e.g. 25-40 word sentences, sensory accumulation]            | [ ]                      | [ ]                           |
```

Default pressure map. Confirm it against the anchors; where the comps do otherwise, follow the comps and record the passage.

| Pressure | Direction |
|---|---|
| Action | toward the fastest end |
| Dread | toward the slowest end while it builds; toward the fastest at the break |
| Grief | toward the slowest end; if the POV character's card says they go numb under pressure, short flat sentences on the fast side instead |
| Comedy | set-up at the baseline; payoff toward the fastest end |
| Exposition | at the baseline; if it must move, toward the fast side, never the slow one, where exposition stalls |

**Precedence.** The writer defaults to the baseline. The writer may move inside the envelope when artifacts/07-outline.md marks scene pressure for that scene or passage, in the direction of the pressure map. The writer never goes outside the envelope.

- A move with no marker is drift. If drafting changes what a scene is, the writer notes the new pressure in work/ and the orchestrator updates the marker; text and marker agree before Phase 4: Adversarial Audit.
- If a scene seems to need cadence beyond an end, the writer stays inside and notes it in work/. Only the orchestrator widens the envelope, in artifacts/05-voice.md, with a new anchored comp passage.

**Outline markers.** Phase 2: Architecture fills each chapter's scene pressure field in artifacts/07-outline.md with the pressure of every scene, in order: rest (the baseline), action, dread, grief, comedy or exposition (for example `rest, dread, action, grief`). Each chapter carries at least one marker toward each end of the envelope, or one line in the outline saying why not (a single-scene chapter, for example). The markers replace any separate "speed variation" or "reading speed" rule: variation is planned in the outline and executed inside the envelope.

**Voice under pressure lives inside the envelope.** The break modes on the voice cards (3.4) must be reachable between the two ends. If a planned break needs more, widen the envelope in Phase 1: Foundation or Phase 2: Architecture, with an anchor, or redesign the break. Nobody widens it mid-draft.

**Local limits** (house defaults; the anchors' measurements override them):

- A run of five or more short sentences is deliberate staccato and lasts one paragraph at most.
- A run of three or more long sentences is legato; it belongs to reflection, not to action.
- Five paragraphs of similar length in a row read as monotony; a one-line paragraph among long ones lands as impact.
- One-word paragraphs: two in the whole book at most.

**The rough sentence.** One deliberately rough sentence per chapter, in a quiet passage, never at the climax. Grammatical, plain, a little clumsy; not clever, not quotable. "The coffee was bad and she drank all of it." "He said okay and then he said it again." "It was a Tuesday or maybe a Wednesday." It breaks polish, not the contract: it may jar against its paragraph, but it stays inside the envelope at the pressure of its passage. The writer may place it while drafting; in Phase 5: Revision Loop the revision editor adds one only if the chapter has none. Nobody polishes it away: if unsure whether a rough sentence is deliberate, leave it.

**Multiple POVs.** One baseline per POV narrator, each with its own anchor. The envelope is shared unless a POV card needs a wider end.

**Dialogue.** The contract governs narration. Each voice card governs its character's speech cadence (and a POV character's direct thought), so a character's lines can run faster or slower than the narration around them.

**Detection test** (Phase 4: Adversarial Audit, Phase 5: Revision Loop). Take five passages from scenes with different markers, one of them marked rest, and measure each with the anchors' unit and method.

- Cadence indistinguishable across the five: the contract was not honoured (a flat voice).
- A passage beyond an envelope end: the contract was broken.
- Baseline passages drifting from the baseline measurement: drift.

Report locations and measurements. The fix is a revision ticket against the contract, never a new rhythm.

### 3.4 Voice cards

A full card for every POV character and every character who speaks in two or more scenes; a mini card for everyone else who speaks.

```
### [Name]
Speech: sentence length when speaking [ ]; vocabulary band [education, era, region, profession];
  syntax habit [e.g. "clips, never subordinates" | "spirals, comma-spliced" | questions as statements];
  verbal tics [2-3]
Code-switching: with authority [ ]; with intimates [ ]; with strangers [ ]; when lying [ ]
Never says: [3-5 phrases or registers, each with the reason; the strongest differentiator]
Image source: [where their comparisons come from]; never from [ ]
Under pressure:
  Stress: changes [ ]; stays [ ]
  Overwhelm: changes [ ]; stays [ ]
  Collapse: changes [ ]; the one tic that survives [ ]
  Recovery: how they sound afterwards (not back to normal) [ ]
Lines: at rest "[ ]"; under pressure "[ ]" (visibly different)
```

Break modes to choose from: syntax fragments; hyper-control (more formal under stress, not less); humor vanishes, or appears where it was absent; they address someone who is not there; present tense invades a past-tense narrative; repetition, the same phrase slightly changed; flat numbness, deliberately under-described. A voice that sounds the same at emotional 2 and at emotional 10 is a template, not a person.

Mini card: role; the one speech habit that identifies them; vocabulary source; one tic; one thing they never say, and why; their voice under pressure in one sentence.

### 3.5 Cover-the-name test

Strip names and tags from a dialogue passage, or line up five lines per character: a reader must be able to tell who is speaking. Pre-solve it in Phase 1: Foundation with a differentiation matrix:

```
| Character | Sharpest marker | Nearest character | How they differ even there |
```

Pass: every pair of speaking characters differs on at least three markers, and no two share their sharpest marker. If two do, push them apart before Phase 1: Foundation closes. Run the same test on real chapters in Phase 4: Adversarial Audit and Phase 5: Revision Loop.

### 3.6 Benchmark passages

Write 3-5 short passages (one to three paragraphs each) in the book's voice, never copied from a comp: at least one at the baseline, one with the voice breaking under pressure, and one carrying an irrelevant thought from a noise profile. Label each with its pressure. They are the tuning fork the writer reads before each chapter.

### 3.7 Drift check, every writing session

Over long sessions the voice drifts back toward the model's default.

1. Before writing: reread artifacts/05-voice.md and the last chapter written.
2. After each chapter: compare its first and last paragraphs. Same voice?
3. With an author sample: open three random paragraphs. Would the author say this? Did a never-used word appear? Do the tics appear at the right rate (too many is caricature, too few is generic)?
4. If the voice drifted, find where it started and fix it before moving on.

## 4. Emotional curve (artifacts/06-emotional-curve.md)

### Seven primary emotions

Every scene evokes at least one.

| Emotion | Use for | In excess causes |
|---|---|---|
| Tension | conflict, threat, uncertainty | fatigue |
| Hope | progress, connection, possibility | naivety |
| Break | loss, devastating revelation, failure | the reader's own depression |
| Relief | partial resolution, humor, rest | complacency |
| Catharsis | emotional climax, truth confronted | melodrama |
| Curiosity | mystery, withheld information, promise | frustration |
| Discomfort | uncomfortable truth, secondhand shame | rejection |

### Chapter map

```
| Ch | Dominant → secondary | Start → end emotion | Peak moment (specific) | Temperature 1-10 | Anchor | Abandonment risk |

High-impact beats: [3-5, each with its setup chapters and payoff chapter; mark re-read rewards]
Residue: [the reader's state ten minutes after the last page]
```

One row per planned chapter. Temperature (10 is devastating) is a planning tool for the shape of the curve. The anchor is the concrete image, moment or line the reader should carry away; the writer drafts toward the anchor, never toward a number.

Variation rules:

- Never three consecutive chapters with the same dominant emotion.
- After a peak (temperature 8-10), the next chapter breathes (4-6).
- Catharsis works only with investment: the reader must care before they suffer.
- The most devastating chapter is never the last; the last needs resolution, even partial.
- Name the residue, the specific state the reader should be in ten minutes after the last page, and design the final chapter backward from it.

Check the overall shape against the market-level findings on emotional oscillation and arc shapes in references/patterns/structure-and-emotion.md instead of restating them here.

### High-impact beats

Name the 3-5 moments the reader will remember. They are the book's selling points and feed Phase 7: Editorial Package. Each needs setup at least two chapters before the payoff. Mark any beat that is also a re-read reward (a detail that means more once the ending is known), so revision does not cut it.

### Investment check

Before any devastating beat:

1. Has the character been shown vulnerable? (The reader feels empathy.)
2. Has the character been shown trying? (The reader respects the effort.)
3. Does the reader understand what is at stake?

If any answer is no, the beat will not land: move it or add setup. In the opening chapters, vulnerability comes before competence: if three or more pages of competence pass without a moment of doubt, weakness or need, add one (the research on vulnerability and empathy is in references/patterns/structure-and-emotion.md).

### Pacing rules the outline inherits

Phase 2: Architecture applies these when it builds artifacts/07-outline.md.

- **Chapter length varies.** If every chapter is the same length, the book is a metronome. After a long, dense chapter, a short, dry one lets the reader breathe. House default: the shortest chapter runs at least 40% under the mean and the longest no more than 50% over it.
- **Scene and reflection alternate.** Fiction: never three action scenes in a row without a pause; never two reflections in a row. Memoir and non-fiction: never three data points in a row (it becomes a report); never three personal reflections without narrative or data (it becomes a diary).
- **Density comes from the comps.** Chapter length and the balance of scene, reflection and data are read from the comps in artifacts/01-market-map.md, never from a genre table.
- **End-of-chapter diagnosis.** Would the reader turn the page? Does the chapter carry marked pressure toward both envelope ends (Rhythm contract)? Does intensity get a breath after it? Does the length earn its content (5,000 words that could say the same in 3,000 get cut)?

## 5. Integration and the Phase 1: Foundation gate

- The character embodies the theme: the protagonist's wound is the book's question.
- The emotional curve reveals the character: the highest-impact moments are where the wound is touched.
- Emotion tests the theme: catharsis comes when the question is confronted, not answered.

Integration test: swap the book's theme for another. If the protagonist loses meaning, the integration holds. If not, the wound and the question are disconnected: reconnect them.

Phase 1: Foundation is done when:

- [ ] every main character has all seven layers, at least two contradictions and a noise profile; secondaries in three or more chapters have mini profiles
- [ ] the theme is a question woven at four levels, with 2-4 motif cards
- [ ] artifacts/05-voice.md holds the full Rhythm contract: a baseline and both envelope ends, each anchored to a named comp and a location (or marked `no comp evidence yet`, with the gap listed as an open question), plus the pressure map
- [ ] every character who speaks in two or more scenes has a full voice card, and the differentiation matrix passes
- [ ] 3-5 benchmark passages exist
- [ ] the emotional curve covers every planned chapter and obeys the variation rules; 3-5 high-impact beats have setup
- [ ] the integration test passes

When something changes later, update the artifact and tell the orchestrator, who records the change in PROJECT_STATE.yaml.
