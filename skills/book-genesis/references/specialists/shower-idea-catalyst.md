# Shower-Idea Catalyst & Word-of-Mouth Design

Turn a raw, unstructured idea ("shower idea", one-line "what if", or voice-note ramble) from a creative author with no formal writing background into a high-concept, emotionally magnetic story engine — without jargon, without hijacking their spark, and without promising sales or virality.

When to load: Phase 0: Intake (orchestrator), alongside `references/specialists/research.md`. Never forward to blind readers.

## Core rule

A beginner author does not need to know beat sheets, narrative theory or publishing terminology. Speak in plain, vivid story choices in the author's own language. Do all the structural translation behind the scenes.

## Step 1: Crystallize three high-concept angles (check-in mode) or select the sharpest (autonomous mode)

Never flatten a weird, vivid idea into a generic genre template. Keep the author's exact original spark, then test what makes a stranger stop scrolling or tell a friend about the book:

1. **The 15-second pitch ("tell-a-friend sentence").** Could a reader explain why this book is unmissable in one breath, without saying "it's about finding yourself"? Test for: a vivid contradiction, a ticking stake, or an unforgettable "what if".
2. **Recognizable shelf entry + fresh subversion (tropes with a twist).** Readers discover books through familiar emotional promises (found family, locked-room mystery, forbidden bargain, second chance, unreliable memory, slow-burn betrayal) and recommend books because of the unexpected turn that makes *this* one unforgettable. Name 2–3 shelf-legible tropes the idea naturally carries, plus the exact subversion that avoids cliché.
3. **Emotional promise.** Name the specific feeling the reader is buying (ache, creeping dread, obsessive curiosity, righteous fury, bittersweet catharsis, gallows humor).
4. **First-10% retail sample hook.** Most digital buyers decide inside the free preview (roughly the first 10% of the book, usually chapters 1–2). Ensure the premise's central question, emotional stake and distinctive voice land before that cutoff.
5. **Organic shareability peaks (not gimmicks).** Identify 2–3 earned story moments — a devastating reversal, a moral dilemma readers will argue about, or an image/choice that haunts the ending — rooted strictly in character wound and causality, never bolted on for shock value.

### In check-in mode (interactive beginner flow)

Before locking `artifacts/00-brief.md`, present **3 distinct directions** for the author's shower idea in plain language (zero technical jargon), each in 4 short lines:
- Working title idea
- The 1-sentence "tell a friend" hook
- The emotional vibe and closest shelf feel
- The twist or central dilemma

Ask one simple question in the author's language: *"Which direction feels closest to the book in your head — 1, 2, 3, or a mix of them?"* Once the author replies (or says "you choose"), lock the brief and proceed without burying them in technical files.

### In autonomous mode

Evaluate 2–3 internal angles, choose the sharpest high-concept direction that preserves every explicit author constraint, record the rationale in `ASSUMPTIONS.md`, and write the chosen design into `artifacts/02-story-engine.md`.

## Output additions inside `artifacts/02-story-engine.md`

Include a concise `## High-concept & word-of-mouth engine` section containing:

- **15-second pitch:** one plain, vivid sentence a reader would repeat to a friend.
- **Shelf tropes & subversion:** 2–3 recognizable tropes and how this book turns each one fresh.
- **Emotional promise:** the core feeling the book must deliver.
- **First-10% sample promise:** what lands inside chapters 1–2 so a sample reader commits.
- **Debatable dilemma & organic shareability peaks:** 2–3 character-driven moments readers are likely to discuss or quote (hypotheses to execute in craft, never a guarantee of virality).

## Honesty boundary

High-concept clarity and emotional resonance raise the odds that readers finish and talk about a book, but no story formula guarantees virality, BookTok traction or bestseller status. Record all positioning choices as creative hypotheses.
