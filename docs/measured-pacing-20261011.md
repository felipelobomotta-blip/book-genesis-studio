# Measured chapter positions for read-only reviews

Trial 30's original native audit placed a chapter-10 decision at approximately
96% of the whole manuscript and used that position in a high-severity structural
finding. The exporter instead measures chapter 10 from word 33,502 through 37,551
in a 43,740-word draft: 76.594% through 85.850%. Whatever its precise paragraph
position, that event cannot occur at 96% of this book. The original audit remains
unchanged. Its separate qualitative pacing claims still require a fresh reading;
arithmetic alone does not settle them.

The neutral production source now supplies cumulative word and percentage
intervals for every chapter, using the exporter's existing visible-prose count.
It excludes headings, formatting separators and hidden comments under the same
counting rules as delivery. It counts the complete supplied snapshot and retains
the existing refusal of partial, duplicate or unrelated chapter lists.

Audits, historical-issue followups and revision plans already receive this source.
Rubric evaluators receive only its measured total and chapter intervals, without
the production contract or any private quality targets. The change does not add
a model call. Explicit
guidance distinguishes chapter-local positions from whole-book positions. Outline
beat percentages remain design estimates, not automatic grounds for a defect.
The additional intervals are compact measured facts, not a new grading policy.
Interpretation and the accuracy of a critic's later narrative remain model
judgments; the controller does not claim these facts guarantee correct reviews.

Regression tests cover unequal chapter lengths, an arithmetic fixture reproducing
the observed chapter-10 interval, recalculation after legitimate edits and source
preservation. Existing integration checks bind current production facts in actual
review, rubric and issue packets through revision, export and completed resume.
All 482 repository tests passed in 461.274 seconds and `verify-suite` passed.
Fresh native book validation remains required before acceptance.
